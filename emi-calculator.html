The EMI formula

For a loan of P, a monthly interest rate r (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n monthly instalments:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)

Worked example

A loan of Rs 10,00,000 at 12% a year for 5 years (60 months) has a monthly rate of 1%. The EMI is Rs 22,244 a month. Over 60 months you pay Rs 13,34,667 in total, of which Rs 3,34,667 is interest.

Tips before you borrow

  • Compare loans using the total payment, not just the EMI.
  • Ask whether the rate is fixed or floating, and how often it can change.
  • Check processing fees, early-repayment charges and insurance requirements.

Frequently asked questions

What is an EMI?

EMI stands for equated monthly instalment: the fixed amount you pay each month so that a loan, including interest, is fully repaid by the end of the tenure. Each payment covers that month's interest first, and the rest reduces the principal.

What is the difference between reducing-balance and flat-rate interest?

With reducing-balance interest, interest is charged only on the amount still owed, so it falls as you repay. A flat rate charges interest on the original loan amount for the whole tenure, which costs far more for the same quoted percentage. This calculator uses reducing balance. For the example above, a 12% flat rate over 5 years would cost about Rs 6,00,000 in total interest instead of Rs 3,34,667.

Why does my bank's EMI differ slightly from this result?

Banks may round differently, charge processing or insurance fees that are not part of the EMI, use a floating rate that changes over time, or count days in the first period. Treat the calculator as a close estimate and confirm the exact figure in your loan offer.

What do the compounding options mean?

The default, "Monthly (standard bank EMI)", divides the annual rate by 12 and applies it each month, which is how most reducing-balance loans are quoted. The semi-annual and quarterly options convert the annual rate to an equivalent monthly rate for loans quoted that way.

Does a longer tenure reduce my cost?

A longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI but increases the total interest paid. Try changing the tenure above and compare the "Total Interest" figure.
