Advanced taxation — focus notes for professionals

Orientation notes for practitioners and ACCA-oriented professionals. Not a substitute for the current tax law, IRD directives, or ACCA ATX syllabus for a given session.

How to use these notes

Advanced tax work is about issue spotting, linking rules, and clear computation layout. Memorising isolated rates without structure fails both exams and client files.

Professional habits that transfer to ATX-style thinking

  • Separate tax base questions from accounting profit (adjustments, disallowables, timing).
  • Document assumptions (residency, permanent establishment, related parties).
  • Order workings: identify chargeable amounts → apply reliefs → rate → credits → admin deadlines.
  • Ethics: distinguish tax planning from non-compliance; keep advice within professional rules.

Nepal practice lens (workplace)

  • Track IRD notices, withholding obligations, and filing calendars for the entities you serve.
  • Reconcile accounting profit to taxable profit with a standing schedule — not only at year-end scramble.
  • VAT and income tax interactions on adjustments need dual review where both apply.
  • Related-party and director transactions attract scrutiny; paper substance and approval.

Exam / study discipline (ACCA orientation)

  • Always use the finance act / examinable rules for your session — rates change.
  • Practise narrative recommendations (when the syllabus requires advice, not only numbers).
  • Build a personal error log: which adjustments you miss under time pressure.

These notes stay high-level on purpose. Detailed rates and reliefs must come from current official sources and your study text for the sitting you are entering.

Worked example: from accounting profit to taxable profit

A company reports accounting profit before tax of Rs. 3,200,000. On review, depreciation charged in the accounts is Rs. 450,000, while the tax-allowable depreciation (per the relevant capital allowance rules) is Rs. 620,000. Entertainment expenses of Rs. 80,000 are disallowable for tax purposes, and the company also has Rs. 150,000 of exempt dividend income already included in accounting profit.

AdjustmentAmount
Accounting profit before tax3,200,000
Add: depreciation (accounting)450,000
Less: tax-allowable depreciation(620,000)
Add: disallowable entertainment80,000
Less: exempt dividend income(150,000)
Taxable profit2,960,000

The exam and practical value here isn't the arithmetic — it's the ordering and labelling. A marker (and a reviewing manager) wants to see each adjustment clearly identified as an add-back or deduction, not a single unexplained net figure. The same discipline applies to a real client file: a reviewer should be able to trace every adjustment back to a specific rule or disallowable category without asking you to explain it verbally.

Where this sits in the ACCA syllabus

This reconciliation logic is introduced at Taxation (TX) and becomes significantly more layered at Advanced Taxation (ATX), where questions combine multiple taxpayers, group structures, and planning scenarios — examiners specifically reward candidates who structure workings clearly and flag assumptions, since ATX marks are earned as much for approach as for the final number.

Frequently asked

Why isn't accounting depreciation simply used for tax?

Because accounting depreciation reflects management's estimate of an asset's economic consumption, which varies by policy choice — tax law instead applies a standardised allowance so that businesses using different depreciation policies aren't taxed inconsistently for the same type of asset.

How do I stay current when rates and reliefs change every year?

Treat the finance act for your session (or the current IRD directive for practice) as the only source of truth for rates — these notes intentionally stay conceptual so they don't go stale, but a specific percentage or threshold always needs checking against the current official text before you use it.
