How the calculation works

Money earns interest on interest, so a sum grows exponentially. The calculator uses the standard compound-interest relationship, where r is the annual rate as a decimal and n is the number of years.

Future value (FV) = PV × (1 + r)^n
Present value (PV) = FV ÷ (1 + r)^n

Worked example

Rs 1,00,000 invested at 10% a year for 5 years grows to Rs 1,61,051. Working backwards, Rs 1,00,000 received in 5 years is worth only Rs 62,092 today at a 10% discount rate.

Where you will use it

  • Comparing a payment today with a payment later.
  • Discounting cash flows in ACCA Financial Management (NPV and investment appraisal).
  • Checking what a fixed deposit or lump-sum investment may be worth at maturity.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between present value and future value?

Future value (FV) is what an amount today will grow to after earning interest. Present value (PV) is what an amount received in the future is worth today after discounting at a chosen rate. They are the same relationship viewed from opposite ends.

Which rate should I enter?

For savings or investments, use the interest rate you expect to earn. For project appraisal in ACCA Financial Management, use the cost of capital or the discount rate given in the question. Rates are entered as a percentage per year, for example 10 for 10%.

Does this calculator handle monthly or quarterly compounding?

It compounds once per period. For monthly compounding, divide the annual rate by 12 and enter the number of months in the "Number of Years" box. For example, 12% a year for 3 years becomes a rate of 1 and a period count of 36.

Is this the same method used in ACCA exams?

Yes. ACCA Financial Management uses the same compound-interest and discount-factor ideas, normally with printed discount-factor tables. The calculator gives the exact figure, so tiny differences from table-based answers are expected.
