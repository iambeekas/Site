How the calculation works

With a monthly investment M, a monthly rate i (annual rate ÷ 12) and n months, the maturity value is the future value of an annuity due:

FV = M × [((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)

Worked example

Investing Rs 5,000 a month for 15 years at an assumed 12% a year means you put in Rs 9,00,000 and, under that assumption, end with about Rs 25,22,880. The difference of roughly Rs 16,22,880 is the estimated growth.

Using it sensibly

  • Run the calculation at several return rates rather than relying on one.
  • Start early: the length of time usually matters more than the size of each instalment.
  • Compare with a one-off investment using the PV & FV calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is a SIP?

A systematic investment plan (SIP) means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually monthly, instead of investing a large sum once. Regular investing spreads your purchases over time.

How is the maturity amount calculated?

Each monthly amount is assumed to be invested at the start of the month and to earn the expected return until the end of the period. The calculator adds up the growth of every instalment using the future value of an annuity formula.

Is the expected return guaranteed?

No. The return you enter is an assumption, not a forecast. Real investments can earn more or less and can lose value. Use a range of rates, for example a low, medium and high case, to see how sensitive the result is.

Does it include tax, fees or inflation?

No. Charges, taxes on gains and inflation are not modelled, so the real purchasing power of the maturity amount will be lower than the number shown.
